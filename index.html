Overview

Abstract

Continuum manipulators can move through constrained spaces, but tendon friction, material deformation, and contact make precise end-effector placement difficult. A grasping system must still work when the final position and orientation are imperfect.

We combine a support-enhanced granular-jamming gripper with a vision-guided, reinforcement-learning reaching policy. Before evacuation, the membrane and mobile particles deform around the object while an internal support encourages lateral enclosure. Vacuum then locks the grasp and provides a direct path to carry the load. The policy controls reaching through absolute tendon-length commands; grasp formation is passive. The video demonstrates contact-error tolerance, several object shapes, simulation-to-real reaching, varied target heights and positions, and consecutive pick-and-release trials.

System overview

Learned reaching. Mechanical adaptation at contact.

The system separates the job of arriving near an object from the job of forming and holding a grasp.

01 / SENSE

Locate the target

Visual feedback estimates the gripper and target positions.

02 / REACH

Command the tendons

A history-based PPO policy outputs absolute tendon lengths to approach and hold near the object.

03 / CONFORM

Enclose the object

The flexible membrane and grains reshape under contact; the internal rod supports lateral wrapping.

04 / HOLD

Apply vacuum

Jamming stiffens the gripper for lift and transfer. Re-inflation releases the object.

The learned controller handles reaching; the gripper's passive mechanics accommodate the remaining contact error.
